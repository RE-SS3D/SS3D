ui bible

    

    
The look of SS3D's main UI, rebuilt in HTML/CSS from the reference screenshots so it can be tuned in a browser before it moves to UI Toolkit. Sprites and fonts are the real project files, loaded straight from Assets/. Sizes are UI Toolkit reference pixels, so a number here is the number that goes in the USS. Each specimen's reference strip shows its screenshot side by side, or under the UI as an overlay. Each section is a tab: pick one in the bar at the top, or step through them with the links at the bottom.




    
principles

    
What the reference screens agree on. Every component tab follows these rules.

    

        
The world shows throughHUD surfaces are dark and translucent, never opaque. Wells, panels and sheets darken the floor by about half. The one exception is a surface that opens over other UI (a dropdown list, a popover, a tool hint): it's nearly opaque, or the text under it reads through.

        
Active is a line, not a colourThe active hand's outline brightens.

        
One slot, one line weightHands, gear, equipment and the search sheet are the same component. In the strip it's a grey fill with an outline; equipment keeps only a faint fill and the search sheet none (frames). Every outline is 2 px. Corners are a fixed share of each box, so a slot looks the same at any size: 15 % on gear slots and cells, 6/64 on hands and frames.

        
White sprites, alpha for stateEvery glyph is a white sprite. An item draws at full strength and an empty slot's hint at about half.

        
Point back to the sourceAn open container's panel carries a caret aimed at the slot it came from, so nested containers read as a chain.

        
One squared typefaceSquareDeal, lowercase, for titles, tabs and labels. The L / R marks need capitals, so they use Disket Mono. Kode Mono for the radial labels and the export code. The map editor, which has no reference, puts its labels in Disket Mono Bold and its text in Kode Mono (all on trial).

what each one is made of

    
Size in reference px, fill, edge, corner radius and opacity. Each colour is named after the token that holds it; a plain colour that happens to equal a token gets that token's name too (pure black reads as --ui-viewport).

    


    
where they could merge

    
    
        
  1. One panel for the three surfaces. The storage panel (24, 24, 24 at 0.55), the search sheet (30, 30, 30 at 0.53) and the pile plate (16, 20, 16 at 0.54) are the same dark see-through surface in three near-identical greys. Two carry the dark 2 px edge, and the corners are 9.55, 8 and 6. One panel with one fill token, the edge on or off, and one corner rule would cover all three: the storage panel's corner follows its cells (theirs plus the 4 px between them and its edge), and the sheet and plate could do the same.
    2. 
        
  2. One box for every slot and cell. Gear slot, hand, frame, small frame, the sheet's frame, storage cell and pile cell are one box: a 2 px white outline at 0.38, 0.24, 0.16 or none, the grey fill on (gear, hands), faint (equipment) or off, at 88 × 64, 64, 45 or 37 px. Corners are two shares of the box: 15 % on gear slots and cells (9.6 at 64, 5.55 at 37), and the frame slot's 6/64 on hands and frames (6 at 64, 4.22 on the sheet). One slot with size, fill and line-strength variants covers them; storage cells and pile cells would become two of its sizes.
    3. 
        
  3. One flat plate for the menus. Tab, segmented option, secondary button and style tile share --ui-menu-tab (black 0.46); text field, dropdown and export code share --ui-menu-field (black 0.48); the slider track is black 0.45. Tab, segmented option and primary button all turn --ui-menu-selected white when picked. That's one sharp-cornered plate with idle and selected, and the three blacks could be one.
    4. 
        
  4. Loose values. The selected style tile's grey fill and edge (120 at 0.42, 205 at 0.75), the slider handle (42, 38, 40 at 0.96), the scroller (black 0.5) the 3 px corners on tiles, scroller and handle, and the sheet's 8 px and plate's 6 px corners are the values in the table without a token.
    5. 
        
  5. The radial stays its own. Ring, wedge and hub share nothing with the rest: it's the only round HUD element and the only light HUD surface.
    6.

storage panel

    
One panel per open container, anchored above the slot it came from, with a caret pointing down at it. A container opened from inside a panel stacks another panel above, which makes the chain from the reference. Click a container (the toolbox in hand, or the one in the panel) to close or reopen its panel.

radial menu

    
The reference's three verbs on a six-direction wheel, 60° apart. Drawn in code, no sprites: the disc is a round element with a thick dark border, the hub a warm grey circle in its hole, and the wedge a 60° band built from two arcs. Move the pointer around the disc.

    

        
.radial-interaction-menuRadialInteractionMenu.uss

        

        
    
            
  • The disc and hub are plain USS (border, radius, colour). The wedge is the one part that needs code: an SVG path here, and generateVisualContent with Painter2D in Unity, using the same two arcs.
    • 
            
  • Enter has no interaction icon. Heroicons' Outline/Logout, turned around, is the reference's glyph.
    • 
            
  • Icons and labels are smaller than the reference's: each icon is sized from its drawn area to look about 24 px, inside the same 32 px slot, and the labels are Kode Mono (on trial) at 11 px. Every verb's icon centre and label sit at the same spot in its block, and the block is centred on the band between hub and rim.

The map editor (B), redrawn with nothing but the bible's parts. The layout, tools, tabs and palette are the shipped MapEditorView's, and the palette is the real catalog.
